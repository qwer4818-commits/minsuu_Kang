Problem Definition

Given a photo of a waste item, classify it into one of six categories — plastic, paper, glass, metal, styrofoam, or trash.

The approach

Dataset & method.

We built it end to end — from camera lens to convolutional kernel.

Collecting the data.

1,057 raw images, sourced three ways. Augmentation balanced the set to 300 per class — 1,800 in total.

Sources

What we learned

Discussion.

The model works. The lessons go beyond the metrics.

✦

Lessons learned

  • Data collection was the hardest part — gathering clean images took longer than training the model.
  • Augmentation can rebalance counts, but it can't create real visual diversity.
  • Transparent classes (plastic vs. glass) defeat a simple from-scratch CNN.
↗

Future improvements

  • Use transfer learning (ResNet, EfficientNet) for a stronger baseline.
  • Collect 1,000+ diverse images per class — varied lighting and angles.
  • Deploy as a mobile app with real-time inference.
  • Extend to multimodal input — RGB + weight or material sensors.

Where it could go.

Smart recycling bins at public stations could classify waste at the moment of disposal, giving instant feedback on incorrect sorting. A consumer app could teach better habits at home.
